# The angle rule (generated)

> Generated by `tools/angle_atlas.py` from the offset rule in `tools/gop_engine.py` (`orb_offset`). See [05 §2.3](../05-tick-engine.md#23-the-angle-rule-where-a-click-sends-an-orb).

## Which of the five angles a click uses

The orb `O` is in the middle. Every other tile is a place you could stand. The letter says which bucket the server picks from the slope |dy/dx| between you and the orb:

| Letter | Slope | Orb is sent (relative to itself) |
|---|---|---|
| `H` | below 1/2 | 2 tiles straight along x |
| `h` | 1/2 up to (not incl.) 1 | 2 along x, 1 along y |
| `X` | exactly 1 | 2 and 2 (pure diagonal) |
| `v` | above 1, up to 2 | 1 along x, 2 along y |
| `V` | above 2 | 2 straight along y |

Because the buckets are cut at slopes 1/2, 1 and 2, an orb can only ever be sent in 16 headings. Counting both signs: 4 straight (`H`/`V`), 4 pure diagonals (`X`) and 8 "knight-like" ones (`h`/`v`). **That is the complete list of angles in GOP.** Every named angle is a way of standing so that one of these 16 headings carries the orb into the scoring zone without clipping scenery.

## Where the orb actually goes on open ground

You `P` hold attract on an orb that starts on the tile shown. Each tile shows the heading of the orb's first step: `-` and `|` straight, `/` and `\` diagonal. An orb sent (1,2) steps diagonally first, then straight. This is what gives the 'dogleg' paths players learn to read.

## Repel

Repel uses the same buckets with the direction reversed (no anti-overshoot clamp). You can click an orb up to 8 tiles away with the repeller, but the push is cancelled when the orb is 8 or more tiles away, so in practice a repel only moves orbs up to 7 tiles from you.

## Line of sight is not mirror-symmetric

The emulator's line-of-sight routine walks a Bresenham-style line and has two hard-coded special cases (runs of 6 tiles with rise 1 or 5, and runs of 10 with rise 1, 3, 7 or 9). Rays to mirror-image targets east and west of you pass through different tiles, so an obstacle can hide an orb on one side but not the other:
